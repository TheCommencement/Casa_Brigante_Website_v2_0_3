October's Change

Some futures can’t be built until the past is finally confronted.

Twenty-one years after walking away from college, October Greene returns to finish the degree she never got and chase the career she’s worked her entire life to earn. But stepping back onto campus means reopening old wounds she spent decades trying to bury.

As buried truths resurface, fractured family relationships are tested, and the weight of choices made long ago refuses to stay in the past, October finds herself fighting for far more than a diploma. She’s fighting for forgiveness, for identity, and for the chance to finally become the woman she was always meant to be.

October’s Change is a deeply emotional novel about ambition, motherhood, friendship, redemption, and discovering that it’s never too late to reclaim your life.

She wasn’t here for vibes. She was here for vengeance.
She was used to being a spectacle. Used to being watched. But this time, it wasn’t validation she was after, it was goddamn reclamation.

From the opening pages

Chapter One · Reclamation Mode

October stepped onto campus like she ain’t have time for the bullshit. Because she didn’t. Not when Cassandra fucking Vale said she needed that degree to even be considered for VP. Not when October was pushing forty in a world that kept tryna play her small. She wasn’t here for vibes. She was here for vengeance.

That Ann Arbor wind hit her like a slap with good manners, just enough chill to make her nipples react, but not enough to stop her stride. Heels slicing through sidewalk cracks. Hair dancing with every step. Waist snatched. Walk tight. Face saying, don’t ask me shit.

Kids were everywhere. Soft-faced, coffee-cupped, laughing like life didn’t owe them rent yet. Some had pajama pants on. One girl had on a bonnet, a blanket, and a lanyard like she was still in high school. A boy on a skateboard almost fell staring at her ass. She didn’t even turn to make sure he was okay.
